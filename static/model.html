Model & methodology

How the forecasting model was built, tuned, compared, and evaluated — including the diagnostic work behind its final reported accuracy.

Process

Data audit

Merged eleven catchment rainfall series with 26 years of daily storage; verified date continuity, checked for nulls, negatives, and out-of-range values.

Duplicate resolution

Found and removed an exact-duplicate catchment: Labukele and Hope Estate returned identical rainfall for all 25 years, traced to both sitting inside the same reanalysis grid cell (9.7 km apart).

Resolution-shift documentation

Identified a 2017 change in the underlying rainfall data's spatial resolution, collapsing ten catchments into three independent signals before that date. Flagged rather than discarded.

Feature engineering

Antecedent rainfall (3/7/14/30-day sums, decay-weighted index), storage momentum, seasonal encoding, and a statistically significant weekly cycle tied to hydropower release scheduling.

Baseline model

XGBoost gradient-boosted trees on the engineered features, time-based train/validation/test split, benchmarked against a naive persistence baseline.

Tuning & diagnosis

Time-series cross-validated hyperparameter search plateaued around NSE 0.47–0.48. Residual analysis traced the ceiling to managed reservoir releases not present in the rainfall data.

LSTM comparison

A sequence model over the 30-day rainfall history, merged with same-day forecasted rainfall and reservoir state, outperformed the gradient-boosted baseline on validation.

Test evaluation & diagnosis

One-time evaluation on the held-out 2024–2025 test set. Traced the majority of residual error to Cyclone Ditwah, November 2025 — see below.

Architecture comparison

Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) — the standard hydrological model evaluation metric. NSE > 0.50 is conventionally considered a satisfactory simulation.

What the test set was dominated by

A small cluster of atypical days dominates the test-set error — not a systematic model weakness. Use the toggle above to see the difference directly.

28 November 2025, Cyclone Ditwah. The model's largest single-day error (predicted +15.9 MCM vs an actual +62.2 MCM rise) falls exactly on the landfall date of Cyclone Ditwah, which brought Sri Lanka's most severe flooding in decades, with landslides recorded specifically in Kotmale East, Nuwara Eliya District. This day and the two days either side of it (27–29 November 2025) are 3 of the 5 highest-error days in the test set. Two further high-error days elsewhere in the test period (4 August 2025 and 27 November 2024) are not associated with this event and were not investigated further. Together these 5 days (0.7% of 731) account for 72% of total squared error; excluding them lifts test NSE from 0.455 to 0.626.

Limitations

Two factors bound this model's achievable accuracy, independent of architecture: managed hydropower releases are not present in any available dataset and inject variance no rainfall-only model can predict; and rainfall data before 2017 has materially coarser spatial resolution than data from 2017 onward, limiting how much catchment-specific signal the model could learn from sixteen of its twenty-six training years.
